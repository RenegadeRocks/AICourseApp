# Week 11 briefs — Define & Validate Your Product Idea + Market/User Validation

Curriculum sessions: "Define Your Product Idea - Validate idea using AI" +
"Market & User Validation - Interview or poll potential users". July-2026
interpretation: evidence-driven validation for AI products — combining
classic discovery (Mom Test-class interviewing) with what's genuinely new in
2026: AI-assisted desk research, synthetic-user simulation AND its serious
epistemic limits, and validation signals specific to agent products (usage
depth, not signups). b0w03 taught problem-discovery frameworks for CLIENT
work — wikilink it; this week is validating YOUR OWN product idea, where
motivated reasoning is the enemy.

Day plan:

- **01-mon — Idea definition: from itch to falsifiable bet.** Problem/customer/
  wedge one-pager, the riskiest-assumption ranking (desirability/viability/
  feasibility for agent products — feasibility is rarely the risk in 2026;
  desirability usually is), kill criteria BEFORE evidence gathering (link
  b0w03 kill-criteria canonical), the "vitamin vs painkiller vs fine-you're-
  fired" test for agent products.
- **02-tue — AI-assisted desk validation.** Deep-research workflows for market
  sizing (bottom-up only; TAM theater called out), competitor/adjacent-tool
  mapping, demand signals (search trends, community pain mining — Reddit/
  Discord/forums with consent-aware scraping per b3w08 ethics — wikilink),
  pricing-comparable research; where AI desk research systematically misleads
  (recency gaps, SEO-slop inputs, sycophantic synthesis — name the failure
  modes and defenses).
- **03-wed — Talking to humans: interviews that don't lie to you.** Mom-Test
  discipline applied to agent products (past behavior > hypotheticals; "would
  you use this?" is worthless), recruiting 10 real prospects without an
  audience (b1w02 niche canonical — wikilink), interview guides, note-taking
  with AI transcription + the consent/recording rules (b3w07 thu canonical for
  consent law — wikilink), synthesis without cherry-picking (tag evidence
  for/against each assumption).
- **04-thu — Synthetic users and simulation: promise and peril.** The 2026
  state of LLM-simulated user research (verify current tools/papers and the
  replication evidence both ways), where simulation helps (interview-guide
  piloting, message testing, segment brainstorming) and where it's dangerous
  (fabricated preference data, sycophancy, distribution collapse — cite the
  research), a defensible protocol: simulate to DESIGN studies, humans to
  DECIDE. This is the week's centerpiece controversy — engage it hard.
- **05-fri — Polls, smoke tests, and the evidence ledger.** Quantitative
  validation: poll design that avoids leading questions, smoke-test tiers
  (waitlist < fake-door < pre-order < paid pilot — escalating evidence
  strength), using the Week-10 launch page as the instrument (wikilink),
  the evidence ledger: every assumption scored against evidence strength,
  pre-registered decision rule (build/pivot/kill), Wilson-interval discipline
  (b2w03 canonical — wikilink, don't re-derive).
- **06-sat — BUILD: run the validation sprint.** A 5-day validation sprint
  plan for the reader's idea: assumption map, desk-research dossier (AI-
  assisted, sources cited), 5 interviews scheduled + guide, one smoke test
  live, evidence ledger with decision rule. code-lab: evidence-ledger tool +
  interview-note synthesizer with for/against tagging (compile-checked).
  Pass bar: a build/pivot/kill decision the reader can defend with evidence.
- **07-sun — Synthesis + quiz + flashcards + Block 4 capstone recap** (how
  W9 package + W10 launch + W11 validation compose into ship-with-evidence).

Controversies (verify current): synthetic users (strong positions both ways in
the research community — name names); "validation theater" critique (talking
to users as procrastination vs the ship-and-see school); AI-assisted research
homogenizing everyone's "insights".
