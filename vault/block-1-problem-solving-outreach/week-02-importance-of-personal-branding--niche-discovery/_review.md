## Cross-cutting observations

**Voice drift — minimal.** Wed switches briefly into "the operator" / "we" voice at Layer 4 (creator-consultant trap pattern); the rest of the week holds second-person-to-the-reader discipline. No drift worth surgical fix.

**Week-level repetition (mild).** Parlance Labs' $89,500 minimum engagement appears in Mon, Wed, Thu, and Sat. The Maven course "3,000+ students from 500+ companies including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google" claim repeats four times. This is reinforcement, not lazy copy, but a skeptical reader scanning the week cover-to-cover will notice the same two data points load-bearing across four lessons. Consider one lesson footnoting others for the Husain data instead of restating.

**Welsh's numbers appear in Mon, Tue, Wed, and Sun** with slightly different cumulative totals ($4.15M 2024; $10M cumulative by mid-2025; 250k+ / 360k+ / 175k-250k newsletter subs depending on measurement date). The lessons flag this ("per more recent sources depending on measurement date") but the reader tracking one operator across the week sees the variance. Sun's flashcards lock numbers harder than the preceding lessons — reconcile before publish.

**L3 vs L2 calibration.** All seven lessons clear L3 bar. The tightest L3 executions are Thu (three-axis × falsifiable-hypothesis frame is a genuine operator contribution, not a rehash of Dunford) and Sat (Dunford-Raskin-Lochhead with honest base-rate math). Mon is the most conventional shape of the week but reads L3 because the Akerlof + BCG + Edelman load-bearing trio is operator-cited with primary sources. Wed is the one lesson that could have slid to L2 — "case study mechanics, three formats" risks being consultant-craft recap — but is pulled up to L3 by the Position-A/B/C steel-man synthesis and the honest Kahl-going-dark 2024 data point.

**Gaps the week does not cover:**
1. **Trust-and-safety / regulatory positioning.** For AI-services operators selling into regulated verticals (legal, finance, healthcare), the brand layer includes compliance-legible signals (SOC 2, model-risk documentation, audit-log artifacts). Week 2 barely touches this; it will matter in Block 2-3 enterprise engagements.
2. **India-specific economics.** Tue has an India surface map; no other lesson translates the brand × niche × pricing economics to India as a sourcing / delivery geography. The cohort's Indian-context operators are under-served.
3. **Non-Western reference operators.** Every named operator in the week is US / EU / indie-Western. A Dhruv Rathee, a Paras Chopra, a Sidu Ponnappa named trajectory would broaden the lesson's credibility to non-Western readers.

**Persona-lens coverage honesty.** The brief invited 5 personas; this review picked 3–5 per lesson. The skeptical-senior-peer lens surfaced the consistent cross-field tax: every lesson except Fri over-indexes on software / ML-engineering reference operators. A marketing director or legal-ops senior reading the week in full sees themselves in the *framework* but rarely in the *case studies*. This is a volume problem, not a depth problem — it lives in which operators get named, not in how the reasoning translates.
