# Week 9 — Packaging & selling your AI agents: the first sellable agent package

## The thesis of this week

Blocks 2 and 3 made you a builder: a sales agent, a report generator, a voice squad, an unattended scraper-summarizer. Every one of those, as it stands, is a *custom service* — it earns money only when you personally scope it, build it, and babysit it for one client at a time. Week 9 converts one of those builds into a **package**: fixed scope, fixed price, repeatable delivery, sellable to strangers. The core move is not adding features. It is *scope collapse*: shrinking the promise until the same build, the same onboarding, and the same runbook serve customer number two through customer number twenty without you rebuilding anything.

This is Block 1's commercial machinery pointed at a different object. [[05-fri-commercial-sow-for-ai-projects|Week 1]] taught you to sell a bespoke engagement to one buyer. This week teaches you to design an offer that sells itself repeatedly: the productized-service-to-product spectrum, tier ladders, guarantees you can actually honor on a probabilistic system, multi-customer delivery engineering, the 2026 distribution surfaces (AgentExchange, AWS Marketplace's agents category, Claude Marketplace, the post-GPT-Store app economy), and a pricing model that survives both the Artisan-style entry-price collapse and a mid-contract model repricing.

## Who this week is for

You have at least one working build from Weeks 4–8 and you have sold, or tried to sell, custom AI work using the Block 1 playbook. You know the [[06-sat-rag-evaluation|eval discipline]] from Week 4 and the [[05-fri-reliability-engineering-for-unattended-agents|reliability engineering]] from Week 8, because a package's guarantees are written on top of both.

## Why these topics belong together

A package is one artifact seen from five angles. Monday decides *what* deserves packaging. Tuesday writes the promise down so a stranger can evaluate it. Wednesday makes the promise deliverable to N customers without N rebuilds. Thursday finds the customers. Friday attaches a number that survives contact with 2026's two-directional model-price curve. Saturday you assemble all of it into something a stranger could buy this month; Sunday you consolidate.

## What "L3 depth" means this week

Every deep-dive engages at least one live controversy with named positions (productized packages vs the snowflake-deployment thesis; guarantees on probabilistic systems; marketplace hope vs direct-sales reality; the usage-based pricing backlash), carries 8+ search-verified citations current to July 2026, includes a runnable experiment with an explicit pass bar, and closes with a reviewer lens arguing against the lesson itself.

## How to study this week

Do Monday's build audit honestly; the whole week compounds on the build you pick. Draft artifacts as you go — tier sheet Tuesday, runbook Wednesday, channel plan Thursday, pricing Friday — so Saturday is assembly, not creation. Run the code-lab before the Saturday live session: the pricing calculator will change what you believe about your own margins.
