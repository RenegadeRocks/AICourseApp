# Week 15 — The public launch motion

## The thesis of this week

[[05-fri-launch-day-instrumentation|Block 4 Week 10]] built the launch *page* and wired it to record a decision. This week is the launch *event* and the outbound motion that drives traffic to that page. The two are different jobs. A page is an artifact you build once; a launch is a demand-generation event you orchestrate on a date, across a portfolio of surfaces, most of which have changed shape since 2024.

The single most expensive belief a 2026 operator can hold is that a launch is a switch: you flip it, the world notices, revenue arrives. It is not a switch. It is one loud day inside a continuous motion, and its main output is rarely a traffic spike — it is credibility assets, a warm list of humans, and a validated read on which two channels deserve your next month. This week teaches the motion honestly, grounded in what Product Hunt, LinkedIn, X, cold email, and AI-answer engines actually reward in mid-2026, not what a 2021 growth thread claims they reward.

## What is new since the field you already know

- **Product Hunt is saturated and editorially gated.** AI's share of launches went from roughly 5% to 40% in under 18 months; "AI-powered" is now noise, not signal; average upvotes per launch fell as attention spread thin; and since 2024 an editorial team curates who gets Featured, with roughly one launch in ten making it.[^1][^2]
- **Cold email deliverability degraded and AI-personalization backfired.** Average reply rates sit near 3.4%, opens fell from ~36% to ~28%, roughly one in five to one in six cold emails never reaches the inbox, and buyers now recognize and penalize generic AI personalization.[^3][^4]
- **AI answer engines became a distribution surface.** Around a third of the US population uses generative search in 2026, and getting *cited* by ChatGPT/Perplexity/AI Overviews (GEO/AEO) is now a discipline distinct from ranking on Google.[^5][^6]
- **The social algorithms already shifted** — LinkedIn's anti-AI-slop demotion and X's Grok/Phoenix ranker are canonical in [[02-tue-content-strategy-and-platform-choice|Block 1 Week 2]]; this week builds the *launch sequence* on top of that state rather than re-teaching it.

## The through-line

Every day answers the same operator question from a different surface: *given a real product and a launch date, where do I spend the scarce hours of that week so that fourteen days later I have revenue signal and a warm list, not just a spike and a feeling?* The Saturday build forces the answer into a plan a stranger could execute on a date.
