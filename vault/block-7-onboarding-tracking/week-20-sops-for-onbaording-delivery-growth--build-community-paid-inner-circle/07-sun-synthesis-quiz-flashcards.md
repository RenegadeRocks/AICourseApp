# Synthesis + quiz + flashcards + Block 7 capstone

## The week in one arc

You started the week with a business that ran on your memory and your presence. You
end it with a business that is written down and has a home. Monday established what
an SOP actually is: a decision made once so it never has to be made again, in
checklist form because checklists beat expertise-plus-memory under pressure
(Gawande's surgical evidence), organized into a hierarchy of onboarding, delivery,
growth, and support. Tuesday zoomed into the highest-leverage SOP category:
onboarding, where time-to-first-value in the first days and locked-in-by-day-30
retention decide whether the customer you paid to acquire actually stays. Wednesday
finished the SOP layer with delivery runbooks and their quality gates, the growth
SOP library, and the SOP-to-agent pipeline that decides which procedures graduate
into automation.

Thursday and Friday built the other durable asset. Thursday made the case that in an
era where features clone overnight, belonging is the moat AI cannot copy, and taught
the free-vs-paid distinction, platform choice on economics, and the cold-start
problem. Friday turned the paid inner circle into numbers: MRR, churn, LTV, the
brutal leverage of churn (halve it, double member lifetime), and the ghost-town
early-warning metric that catches a dying community weeks before the churn shows up
in revenue. Saturday assembled all of it into a runnable SOP library and a
paid-community launch plan, with the `code-lab/1/` tools doing the SOP rendering and
the membership math.

The through-line of the whole week: **a business in your head is a job; a business
written as SOPs and held together by a community is an asset.** SOPs make delivery
and growth repeatable without you; community makes retention structural instead of
heroic. Together they are what "systematized" actually means.

## Quiz (take it cold)

Answer without reopening the lessons. Answer key follows.

1. **(Short answer)** In one sentence, what is an SOP, and why is the checklist form
   preferred over comprehensive prose?

2. **(MCQ)** According to the 2026 benchmarks, customers who hit first value inside
   14 days retain at roughly ___ at month 12, while those who do not reach first value
   within 30 days retain at only ___.
   a) 60% / 40%  b) 80%+ / 35-50%  c) 95% / 70%  d) 50% / 20%

3. **(Short answer)** Name the four parts of a well-formed SOP (beyond the prose of
   the steps).

4. **(MCQ)** Which of these is the *worst* candidate to hand to an agent?
   a) Sending a templated welcome email on a trigger
   b) Deciding whether to fire a difficult client
   c) Pulling the month's metrics from connected sources
   d) Triaging inbound messages by category

5. **(Short answer)** Why is churn described as the "most sensitive input" in
   membership unit economics? Use the LTV relationship in your answer.

6. **(Calculation)** A membership has ARPU of $80/month, a 90% gross margin, and 5%
   monthly churn. What is the average member lifetime in months, and what is the LTV?

7. **(MCQ)** The strongest retention among membership types comes from:
   a) static course-only memberships
   b) chat-only communities
   c) hybrid memberships (community + content + live interaction)
   d) one-time cohort programs

8. **(Short answer)** Explain the difference between a free community and a paid
   community as two different *products* with two different jobs.

9. **(MCQ)** In 2026, Skool's Pro plan and Circle's entry-and-Business tiers are
   priced approximately at:
   a) Skool $9 / Circle $19 & $49
   b) Skool $99 / Circle $89 & $199
   c) Skool $199 / Circle $99 & $299
   d) all three are free

10. **(Short answer)** What is the ghost-town early-warning metric, and why is it
    better than watching churn?

11. **(Short answer)** The lesson says an SOP handed to an agent must be *stricter*
    than one handed to a human. Why?

12. **(Scenario)** Your paid community's MRR is growing month over month, but you
    notice the active-member ratio has fallen for three straight weeks and new members
    are not posting in their first week. What is happening, and what should you do?

13. **(MCQ)** According to the classic 90-9-1 participation rule, what fraction of
    community members typically account for most of the activity?
    a) 90%  b) 50%  c) 9%  d) 1%

14. **(Short answer)** Give the rule for what you should and should NOT systematize,
    and name the dividing variable.

15. **(Short answer)** Why does the free community solve the cold-start problem for a
    paid inner circle?

## Answer key

1. An SOP is a decision made once and written down so it is executed identically
   every time; the checklist form wins because experts fail not from lack of
   knowledge but because complexity outruns memory under pressure, and a verifiable
   checklist externalizes the memory (Gawande's surgical-checklist evidence).[^1]

2. **b) 80%+ / 35-50%.** The TTFV-to-retention correlation is the reason onboarding
   is the highest-leverage SOP.[^2]

3. A trigger (the event that starts it), a checklist of ordered verifiable steps, a
   definition of done *and* of good, and an owner plus a version. (Inputs and failure
   modes are strong additions.)

4. **b) Deciding whether to fire a difficult client.** It is a judgment task; an
   agent would fill the ambiguity with a confident wrong guess. The others are
   deterministic, high-frequency agent candidates.

5. LTV = (ARPU x gross margin) / monthly churn, so LTV divides *by* churn. A small
   reduction in churn produces a large increase in LTV (halving churn doubles member
   lifetime and roughly doubles LTV), which no acquisition tactic matches.[^3]

6. Average lifetime = 1 / 0.05 = **20 months.** LTV = ($80 x 0.90) / 0.05 =
   **$1,440 per member.**

7. **c) hybrid memberships (community + content + live interaction),** which can
   sustain monthly retention above 96% because live interaction is inherently
   recurring and belonging makes leaving a loss.[^4]

8. A free community is a *funnel*: its job is reach, brand, and trust at the top,
   monetized elsewhere, and its currency is scale. A paid community is a *product*:
   its job is revenue and depth, you owe members ongoing value, and its enemy is
   churn. Running one like the other fails both.[^5]

9. **b) Skool $99 (Pro) / Circle $89 (Professional) & $199 (Business).** Discord is
   free; platform fees set the pricing floor.[^6]

10. The active-member ratio (fraction of members active in the last 7 days) and its
    trend, plus new-member first-week activation. It beats churn because churn is a
    *lagging* indicator (a member who cancels disengaged weeks earlier), while
    falling engagement is a *leading* indicator that gives you time to intervene
    before the community dies.[^4]

11. Because a human silently fills ambiguous gaps in a checklist with context and
    judgment, while an agent fills them with a confident guess. So agent-run SOPs need
    failure modes and stop conditions spelled out that a human-run SOP can leave
    implicit.[^7]

12. The community is sliding toward a ghost town; the falling active-member ratio and
    weak first-week activation are the leading indicators, and the MRR growth is
    lagging and will bend once the disengagement converts to churn. Act now: re-energize
    the active core, run an event, get the founder posting again, and fix the
    new-member first-interaction hook, while the community is still saveable.[^4]

13. **d) 1%.** ~90% lurk, 9% contribute occasionally, 1% drive most activity; the
    2026 update is that well-run communities beat this ratio.[^8]

14. Systematize the *repeated and stable* (done the same way five-plus times);
    do NOT systematize the *rare, still-changing, or judgment-defined*. The dividing
    variable is **variance**, not importance: important-but-stable tasks should be
    heavily systematized, important-but-high-variance tasks should not.

15. Because a paid community launched cold to strangers lands in an empty room that
    repels the first visitor, whereas a free community lets you build a living room of
    the right people first, identify your true fans, prove you can sustain engagement,
    and then seed the paid inner circle with a founding nucleus so it is never empty.[^5]

## Flashcards

**1. Q:** What is an SOP, in one line?
**A:** A decision made once and written down so it is executed identically forever after.

**2. Q:** Why does the checklist form beat comprehensive prose?
**A:** Experts fail from complexity outrunning memory, not lack of knowledge; a checklist externalizes the memory (Gawande).

**3. Q:** The four parts of a well-formed SOP?
**A:** Trigger, ordered verifiable checklist, definition of done-and-good, owner + version.

**4. Q:** The four SOP categories in a service business?
**A:** Onboarding, delivery, growth, support/admin.

**5. Q:** How do you sequence which SOP to write first?
**A:** Frequency x pain: the task you do most often and hate most.

**6. Q:** When should you NOT systematize?
**A:** Rare, still-changing, or judgment-defined tasks. The dividing variable is variance, not importance.

**7. Q:** The two distinct ways AI touches SOPs?
**A:** AI writes the first draft (removes blank-page problem); AI runs the routine procedure (SOP as agent spec).

**8. Q:** Why must an agent-run SOP be stricter than a human-run one?
**A:** A human fills ambiguous gaps with judgment; an agent fills them with a confident wrong guess. Spell out failure modes and stop conditions.

**9. Q:** What is time-to-first-value (TTFV)?
**A:** Elapsed time from sign-up/signature to the customer's first experience of the core value.

**10. Q:** The TTFV-to-retention correlation (2026)?
**A:** First value inside 14 days → 80%+ retention at month 12; not within 30 days → 35-50%.

**11. Q:** What predicts ~90% eventual churn?
**A:** No meaningful engagement in the first three days.

**12. Q:** Top-quartile TTFV benchmarks (2026)?
**A:** Under 5 minutes (single-player self-serve); under 24 hours (multiplayer/config-heavy).

**13. Q:** The onboarding design principle?
**A:** Front-load value, back-load information collection. Deliver a quick win before the long form.

**14. Q:** Which onboarding parts to automate vs keep human?
**A:** Automate the plumbing (acknowledgement, setup, activation flags); keep the trust-defining human contact (kickoff, first-value delivery).

**15. Q:** What is a quality gate, and where does its discipline come from?
**A:** A checkpoint against an explicit rubric before work reaches the client; borrowed from AI eval discipline (Week 4).

**16. Q:** The three axes of the SOP-to-agent rubric?
**A:** Frequency (payback), determinism (feasibility), cost of error (safety).

**17. Q:** Why is community the durable moat in the AI era?
**A:** Features clone overnight; belonging, trust, and network effects require real elapsed time to accumulate and cannot be fast-forwarded.

**18. Q:** The Spinks core principle of community?
**A:** Shift from creating all value *for* members to creating spaces where members create value *for each other*.

**19. Q:** Free community vs paid community?
**A:** Free = funnel (reach, currency is scale, monetized elsewhere). Paid = product (revenue and depth, enemy is churn, you owe ongoing value).

**20. Q:** 2026 community platform pricing (Skool / Circle / Discord)?
**A:** Skool $99 Pro (~2.9% fee); Circle $89 Professional & $199 Business (2%/1% fees); Discord free (no built-in monetization).

**21. Q:** The cold-start problem, and its fix?
**A:** An empty community has no value so no one joins. Fix: seed a founding nucleus, be the most active member, start narrow, engineer first interactions.

**22. Q:** The 90-9-1 rule (and its 2026 update)?
**A:** ~90% lurk, 9% contribute occasionally, 1% drive most activity; well-run modern communities push combined engagement above 30%.

**23. Q:** The three ingredients of a strong paid membership?
**A:** Access (to you and peers), ongoing content + live interaction, belonging/identity.

**24. Q:** Which membership type retains best?
**A:** Hybrid (community + content + live), which can sustain >96% monthly retention.

**25. Q:** The membership LTV formula?
**A:** LTV = (ARPU x gross margin) / monthly churn. Average lifetime = 1 / monthly churn.

**26. Q:** Why is churn the most sensitive input?
**A:** LTV divides by churn; halving churn doubles member lifetime and roughly doubles LTV. No acquisition tactic matches that leverage.

**27. Q:** Healthy vs danger monthly churn for a paid community?
**A:** Healthy < 5% (retention > 95%); over 10% churn (below 90% retention) signals a real problem.

**28. Q:** The LTV:CAC healthy benchmark?
**A:** At least 3:1. Below it usually means churn is too high, not acquisition too expensive.

**29. Q:** The ghost-town early-warning metric?
**A:** Active-member ratio (active-in-last-7-days / total) and its trend, plus new-member first-week activation. A leading indicator that moves weeks before churn.

**30. Q:** The Block 7 arc in one line?
**A:** Capture demand (W18) → deliver it reliably (W19) → systematize and compound it with SOPs and community (W20).
